Crash on 14 Oct 2026: what the data shows, and how ThrottleIQ should catch the next one

Rider @theabraarrar · Honda CG 125 · Kamalapur–Tongi railway level crossing beside Banani Overpass, Dhaka · ride aac4e04d · report written 15 Oct 2026

Read this first

  • What happened (confirmed by the rider, 15 Oct). A small jam at a double-track level crossing. The rider crossed the first rail at walking pace. Then, pressed by horns and flashing headlights, they opened the throttle (12.7 km/h and accelerating at 21:28:47) while crossing the second rail at an angle. The rear wheel kicked up and out, and the bike threw the rider to the left: hands first, the bike landing on their foot. That was about 21:28:48. The iPhone's motion wake at 21:28:58.9 was the phone being moved afterwards. Stopped for about 4½ minutes, then rode on.
  • Why the app said nothing. Crash detection is switched off in code (impactDetectorLiveEnabled = false). With the switch off, the detector never runs, and the app saves no accelerometer or gyro samples at all, on the phone or in the cloud. The force of the impact cannot be recovered from anywhere.
  • Even switched on, it would probably have missed. I ran the app's own detector code on this ride's real GPS fixes, with simulated impacts for five phone and bike situations. For a fall at the stop, it flagged the crash in only 2 of the 5. Even then, it needed the impact to land within about 4 seconds of the last GPS fix, and the phone to register at least 4 g. For a tip-over 10 s later, no speed-based rule can catch it at all, today's or proposed, because the bike was already stopped.
  • Six rule changes fix the logic. Five of them were tested on the same data: for a fall at the stop, they flag this crash in all 5 situations at every plausible impact time, with today's GPS settings. The sixth catches a tip-over while stopped: an impact, then the phone stays rotated. Whether the phone registered a big enough knock is still unknown. So the plan adds a lower 2.5 g "ask after the ride" threshold and starts recording sensor data around every impact.
  • The Spark plan is not what blocks detection. Detection, logging and the after-ride question run on the phone and in Firestore, and both work on Spark. Spark only blocks server-side alerts (Cloud Functions). Even on Blaze, an alert would reach nobody today, because the delivery code is a placeholder (DELIVERY_IMPLEMENTED = false).
  • Where the phone sits changes the signal.
    • A handlebar mount feels engine vibration, and after a fall it lies on its side with the bike.
    • A pocket feels a softened body impact, can be thrown out, and follows the rider's posture.
    The app should ask where the phone is at ride start, also guess it from the sensors, and log both.
  • The plan, in order:
    1. Detect silently, with no alarm ("shadow mode").
    2. Save every possible impact with 12 seconds of sensor data.
    3. Ask the rider after the ride whether they fell.
    4. Store the answers privately and, with consent, in one central collection. That collection gives honest numbers for demos.
    5. Calibrate the detector with a safe test plan.
    6. Switch on live alarms last.

Do these first

  1. Split the on/off switch: detection on in shadow mode, live alarm off (sensor_constants.dart:60).
  2. Apply the six rule changes, sample the sensors at 50 Hz instead of 20 Hz, and measure the impact from the raw accelerometer. Add this crash, both versions, as a regression test.
  3. Save each possible impact, with its 12 seconds of sensor data, in a new impact_events table, and sync it with caps.
  4. Add the after-ride "Did you fall?" card, plus "Report a crash" for falls the app missed.
  5. This week, record mount-vs-pocket vibration and phone-drop tests with the free phyphox app. No code needed.

How each piece was worked out

  • When the phone stopped. The app saves every fix that isn't on a steady stretch, and at most one every 5 s on steady stretches (recording_cadence_policy.dart). So any fix after about 21:28:48 would have been saved, and none was until 21:29:02. With the 3 m distance filter, that means the phone moved less than 3 m from about 21:28:48–49 onwards. At 3.5 m/s it must have stopped within a second or two of the 21:28:47 fix. The rest point sits 9.4 m ahead of that fix, but GPS accuracy was 13–14 m, so that gap is mostly GPS error.
  • When the fall happened: two readings.
    • (1) A fall at the stop, about 21:28:48. The front wheel catches or slips on the rail at about 3.5 m/s, and the phone is thrown. The 21:28:58.9 motion wake is then the phone being moved about 10 s later.
    • (2) An abrupt stop at about 21:28:48–49, for example the wheel caught in the rail gap, and then a tip-over from a standstill at about 21:28:59 that throws the phone face-down. Nothing woke the phone at 21:28:48, and a face-down landing explains the covered proximity sensor.
    Resolved by the rider's account: reading (1). They opened the throttle to cross the second rail at an angle, and the rear end kicked out to the left. The data matches: accelerating at 21:28:47, phone still from about 21:28:48–49.
  • GPS quality. Accuracy worsened from 8 m to 11–13 m on the crossing, then to 14–22 m with the phone on the ground. The confidence score fell from 89 to 58. Both are consistent with the overpass and expressway blocking the sky and a phone lying on the road.
  • Heading 343° → 280° across the gap. Between GPS fixes, the app adds up rotation from the gyroscope's z-axis (vehicle_state_estimator.dart:132) and blends in the GPS course. A stationary iPhone usually reports no valid course. So the 63° jump probably reflects the phone rotating as it was thrown. Treat this as weak evidence: issue §78.12 already flags the gyro's axis and sign as unverified.
  • The 9:30 screenshot. The system log puts it at 21:30:11.5–21:30:19.7. That agrees with the window worked out from the ride timer (1h 25m) and the status bar (9:30), 21:30:00–21:30:18. Its 74% confidence matches the saved fix at 21:30:01.
  • Context.
    • Night, and dry. The weather model shows clear skies at 21:00 and only a light drizzle at 14:00–16:00. This is modelled weather for the area, not a station reading.
    • The map shows the rails cutting across the road diagonally, at a shallow angle. Shallow crossings are where front wheels slip or catch.
    • Google Maps navigation was running at the same time.
    • The rider's account: left hand down first, then the bike fell on the rider, and the phone flew out of the trouser pocket.

2. Would the app have caught it?

To answer this, I ran the app's own ImpactDetector code inside a temporary Flutter test, and deleted the test afterwards. The inputs were:

  • GPS: the real GPS fixes from the phone database for 21:27–21:31.
  • Sensors: simulated 20 Hz accelerometer streams, because no real ones exist. The simulation covers five phone and bike situations.
  • Impact timing: 12 times, every 0.5 s from 21:28:47.5 to 21:28:53.0. This covers reading 1, a fall at the stop. Reading 2 is discussed below the chart.
  • Impact size: the phone registering either 3 g or 5 g at the moment of impact.

I tested three versions:

  1. today's rules with today's GPS;
  2. today's rules with one GPS fix per second;
  3. the proposed rules (section 3) with today's GPS.

Reading 2, a tip-over at about 21:28:59. The appendix grid shows every rule set missing impacts from about 21:28:56 on. By then the last GPS fix is more than 10 s old and the bike is already stopped, so no speed-based rule has a "speed before" to work with. This is the gap the sixth rule (section 3) closes: an impact while stopped, followed by the phone staying rotated.

Why today's rules miss

  1. The switch is off (sensor_constants.dart:60), so nothing runs.
  2. The detector only looks back 5 s for the speed before the impact (impact_detector.dart:101, a constant). At walking pace, GPS fixes arrived every 2–9 s, because of the 3 m distance filter (ride_recording_provider.dart:563). Any impact more than 5 s after the last fix has "no speed before", so it can never be confirmed. That's why today's rules only work in the first 4 s after 21:28:47.
  3. GPS silence counts as "stopped" only if the phone is perfectly still (impact_detector.dart:265). An idling engine shaking a mounted phone, or a rider moving in pain, blocks confirmation. The only other route is a GPS fix below 2 m/s within 5 s, and the distance filter usually prevents that fix from arriving.
  4. Falls below about 7 km/h cannot confirm. The rules need a 2 m/s drop down to below 2 m/s (impact_detector.dart:78–79).
  5. A rider lying on their back and moving gives no "settled" signal. The phone isn't still, and a thigh pocket barely rotates between sitting on the bike and lying on the back.
  6. The impact is probably under-measured. The sensors are sampled at 20 Hz (ride_recording_provider.dart:602–609). On iOS the detector reads the phone's motion-filtered, gravity-removed acceleration (sensors_plus 4.0.2, FPPStreamHandlerPlus.swift:104/118). An impact lasts tens of milliseconds, so its peak can fall between samples. The 4 g threshold itself is marked uncalibrated (sensor_constants.dart:46).
  7. The live response is all-or-nothing. A confirmed crash immediately starts a 60 s alarm, marks the ride as crash, and queues an emergency document (ride_recording_provider.dart:1332). That is right for a calibrated detector, but too aggressive for a first version. Hence shadow mode first.
  8. Nothing is kept. Near-misses live only in memory, capped at 50 (impact_detector.dart:10, :103). Today the app could not learn from this crash even if the detector had run.

Caveats. The sensor streams are simulated. The test shows whether the logic confirms the crash, not what force a real phone would read. The saved GPS fixes are thinned on steady stretches (at most one every 5 s). After 21:28:47, though, any unthinned fix would have been saved, so the live detector would have seen essentially the same silence.

Result on this crash. For a fall at the stop, with today's GPS settings and a 5 g reading, rules 1–5 confirm the crash at all 12 impact times, in all five situations. Rule 6 covers the tip-over reading; the implementation's regression test replays that case too. With a 3 g reading, they confirm it only with the 2.5 g ask-later threshold. Impacts during the 4–7 km/h pass over the rails (21:28:26–21:28:45) were tested with 1 fix/s GPS, because today's sparse fixes there contradict an impact. Optional: one GPS fix per second (distance filter 0). This cuts the time to confirmation from about 5 s to about 1 s, and lets today's rules handle situations A, B, D and E. Test it first. The distance safeguard lets each fix add up to its own accuracy radius (fix_kinematics.dart:42), so a phone sitting still could pile up jitter distance in Dhaka jams. Record a 10-minute stationary test with the filter at 0 before shipping it.

4. Three tiers: log, ask, alarm

The detector decides a tier for each impact; the tier decides what the app does with it. The thresholds below are starting points to calibrate, not final values.

This crash under the tiers: probably tier 0. Tier 1 in both readings: through the speed rules for a fall at the stop (speed before 3.5 m/s), or through rule 6 for a tip-over. Tier 2 no: under 15 km/h. That is the outcome we want: a walking-pace fall triggers no emergency alarm, but the app asks about it after the ride.

Safety you can ship without a server:

  • a local alarm with a countdown (tier 2 only);
  • an opt-in lock-screen card for bystanders, showing the rider's Safe QR details (blood group, allergies, emergency contact). It's opt-in because it puts medical information on the lock screen;
  • one-tap Call 999 and Call <contact> buttons on the crash screen.

On Android, auto-calling the first contact after the countdown is technically possible with the CALL_PHONE permission. It still needs a Play policy check and testing for whether calls can start from the background. iOS always asks the user before placing a call or sending an SMS.

Recommendation: do shadow mode and labels now, on Spark. Choose the alert delivery path (Blaze with an SMS provider, or a free external sender for push) only when the detector meets its targets in section 10.

How the app should handle placement

  1. Ask at ride start: one tap on "Phone: Mount · Pocket · Bag". Remember the last answer, and skip the question for auto-detected rides.
  2. Infer it from the sensors as a check, and only log the guess at first:
    • charging while riding (battery_plus is already installed) suggests a mount;
    • a tilt that stays steady for minutes suggests a mount;
    • high-frequency vibration that rises and falls with engine speed suggests a mount;
    • screen on with the app in front (navigation) suggests a mount;
    • a covered proximity sensor suggests a pocket or bag (Android only).
  3. Use placement-aware rules:
    • Mount: the spike threshold adapts to the ride's own vibration, for example ≥ 4 g and ≥ 3× the rolling 99th percentile. Settling needs rotation or stillness.
    • Pocket: free-fall followed by impact counts extra.
    • Bag: a lower tier-0 threshold, with more weight on speed collapse and didn't-ride-on.
  4. Use a vibration-dampening mount. Apple has warned that motorcycle-engine vibration can damage iPhone camera stabilisation, and suggests a vibration-dampening mount. It protects the phone and gives cleaner sensor data.

8. What to log, and where

The five data sets

  1. users/{uid}/impactEvents/{id} (private, one per rider):
    • every tier-0 summary, up to 20 per ride;
    • the 12 s sensor window for tier 1 and 2 events;
    • the window for a random 10% of tier-0 events, to measure false alarms.
  2. crashEvents/{id} (central, with consent, de-identified): one row per labelled event, whether "yes", "no" or "missed". This feeds training and statistics.
  3. A sensor summary on each ride row: the sample rate actually achieved, placement (declared and inferred), vibration level, and candidate counts.
  4. Track v2: accuracy, heading, confidence, period and segment flags for every point. These come from DOCS/gaps/phone_vs_cloud_data.md, so a crash can still be reconstructed after a reinstall.
  5. Labels: the rider's answer, the details and the time.

Window format.

  • 12 s long: 4 s before the impact and 8 s after.
  • 50 Hz, with 9 channels: raw accelerometer xyz, motion-filtered acceleration xyz, and gyro xyz.
  • Stored as int16 (0.01 m/s² and 0.001 rad/s units), then gzipped. That is 10.8 KB before compression.

9. The after-ride question

Possible fall at 9:28 pm
Your phone felt a hard knock and then stopped moving near the rail crossing. Did you fall?

Yes, I fellNo, my phone fellNo, just a bumpNo, I braked hardNot sure

If the answer is "Yes", two optional questions follow:

  • How bad was it? No injury · Minor injury · Needed medical help
  • Surface? Paved · Broken · Dirt · Gravel · Wet · Rail crossing · Speed bump

Phone placement is already filled in from the ride start. Skip and Save are always one tap.

  • When to ask: on the ride summary right after a manual ride, or at the next app open for auto-detected rides. Remind once, then stop asking.
  • Catching misses: add Report a crash or fall on the ride detail screen. The rider picks the moment on the speed graph, prefilled with the biggest stop. Without these reports, recall can't be measured (see section 11).
  • Language: English and Bangla. Bangla strings are machine-drafted and go into bn_pending_review.txt, as the project requires.
  • What each answer saves, always locally plus in impactEvents:
    • "Yes": label crash, or fall if there was no injury and speed was under 10 km/h. It also goes to crashEvents when the rider has consented.
    • "Phone fell", "bump" or "braked": that label, the same way. These are the false-alarm examples that teach the detector.
    • "Not sure": unsure, kept out of the statistics.
    • No answer: unlabelled. The window is dropped after 30 days.
  • Seed the dataset with this crash. Record it as the first labelled event, with label missed because the detector was off: pocket, phone thrown out, about 12.7 km/h, rail crossing, night, dry, CG 125.

11. Honest demo statistics

  • Detected crash: a tier-1 or tier-2 event the rider confirmed with "Yes". Unconfirmed candidates are never counted as crashes.
  • Recall: detected confirmed crashes ÷ (detected confirmed crashes + crashes riders reported as missed).
  • False-question rate: tier-1 questions answered "No", per 100 rides.
  • False-alarm rate: tier-2 alarms the rider dismissed, per 100 ride-hours.
  • Breakdowns: by district, surface, bike class, placement, speed band and time of day. A district is found offline, by matching geohash6 against Bangladesh district boundaries from an open dataset (check its licence).
  • Scripts: scripts/export_impact_events.js and scripts/crash_stats.js use the Admin SDK, following the existing scripts, and output CSV plus a JSON summary. On Spark, reads are free up to 50,000 a day.

A claim you can defend: "In our beta, ThrottleIQ detected N rider-confirmed crashes and falls across D districts, on paved roads, broken roads, gravel and rail crossings. It caught X of the Y crashes riders reported (Z% recall), with W false questions per 100 rides."
