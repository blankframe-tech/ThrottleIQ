🏍️ ThrottleIQ Privacy Policy

Effective date: 1 August 2026

ThrottleIQ is a motorcycle ride-tracking app. It is built and run by one independent developer, and it is currently in beta. This policy describes, in plain terms, exactly what the app collects, where that data goes, and how you get rid of it. It reflects what the shipped code actually does — nothing is described here that the app does not do.

Questions, requests, or complaints: the.abraar.rar@gmail.com. That address reaches the developer directly; there is no support desk behind it.

What the app does not collect

  • No advertising, and no data sold or shared with advertisers or data brokers. Ever.
  • No behavioural analytics. The app does not bundle Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, or any third-party tracker, so there is no usage-tracking profile of you. It does use Firebase Crashlytics to catch app crashes — see the crash-diagnostics row in the table above — but that only fires when the app itself breaks, not to track what you do in it.
  • No push notifications. Notifications you see are generated on your own phone or read from your own account records. Google's messaging library is bundled with the app and, like every Firebase app, the Firebase SDKs give your installation an anonymous identifier — but ThrottleIQ never reads, stores, or sends anything to a push token, and nobody can push a message to your phone through it.
  • No contact list, no SMS, no microphone, no camera roll scanning. Photos are chosen one at a time through your phone's own picker.

2. What actually leaves your phone

ThrottleIQ records offline first. Rides are written to a database on your own device before anything is uploaded, and the app works with no connection at all. When you are back online it syncs to the cloud so that your history survives losing or reinstalling your phone.

What is backed up, and who can reach it

Two things are uploaded for each ride: the summary (date, distance, duration, max speed, event counts, bike) and the point-by-point GPS trail — the full route, with a timestamp, speed and acceleration figure at each point. Your bikes, maintenance logs and saved routes are backed up too.

All of it lands in a private area of the database keyed to your account. Database security rules restrict every one of those records — the GPS trail especially — to your account alone. No other rider can read them, and none of it appears anywhere in the app for anyone but you.

Your route only becomes visible to anyone else if you deliberately make it so: sharing a ride to the feed (§5), publishing a saved route, or starting a live-share link (§6).

Uninstalling the app deletes the local database on the device. The cloud backup survives an uninstall by design — that is what it is for — so uninstalling is not the same as deleting your data. To remove the backup as well, see §9. You can export any ride to a JSON or GPX file from the app if you want your own copy.

Anonymous road-speed data

After each ride, the app also contributes a small, anonymous record for each rough stretch of road you rode: a speed, the day of the week, the hour, and (when available) the weather at the time. This goes into a shared pool that every rider's app draws from — not your account's private area.

These records carry no name, no account ID, no ride ID, and no exact coordinates — only a coarse ~150m map cell, which is not enough to pinpoint an address or identify who rode it. They cannot be linked back to you or your account, by us or by anyone else.

The pool is used for one thing: right after a ride, the app privately compares your own speed on a stretch against the anonymous history for that same stretch, and if yours was a real outlier, shows you a note about it on your own ride summary. Nobody else ever sees this comparison, and it is never reported, published, or used to identify you to anyone — including other riders, insurers, or authorities.

This is on by default for every recorded ride and does not currently have its own off switch separate from turning off ride recording entirely.

Home-screen widgets

If you add one of the app's home-screen widgets, the figures it shows — recent ride stats and upcoming maintenance — are written to your phone's own local app storage so the widget can draw them. That data stays on the device and is not sent anywhere. Bear in mind a widget is visible to anyone who can see your home screen.

3. Background location — why it is needed

ThrottleIQ asks for permission to access your location while the app is in the background, which on Android means the "Allow all the time" setting.

The reason is specific: a ride recording has to keep running when your phone screen is off or another app is in front. On a motorcycle that is the normal case — the phone is on a handlebar mount with the screen locked, or showing a navigation app, for the entire ride. If location access stopped the moment the app left the foreground, the recording would break into fragments, distance and max-speed figures would be wrong, and crash detection would go blind exactly when it matters most.

Concretely, while and only while a ride is recording:

  • The app runs an Android foreground service with a permanent, visible notification reading "Ride Recording Active — ThrottleIQ is recording your ride in the background." You can always see when it is running.
  • Location collection starts when you press start on a ride and stops when you finish it. The app does not track you between rides.
  • The one other use of location is one-off: when you open the Places tab or ask to import nearby places, the app takes a single position fix to find what's around you.

You can refuse or later revoke background location in your phone's system settings. Ride recording will then only work while the app is on screen, which in practice means it will not work reliably. Nothing else in the app depends on it.

5. What other riders can see

  • Your profile — display name, @username, nickname, bio, profile photo, and your email address — is readable by other signed-in riders by default, because riders find each other by exact email or username. You can restrict your profile's visibility to mutual follows, or make it private, in profile settings.
  • Rides are private until you share them. When you share one, you pick the audience: public, your followers, or mutual follows only. A shared ride carries its route line, stats, your name and photo, the bike, and any caption or photo you attached.
  • Route trimming. When a ride is shared, roughly the first and last 200 metres of the route are cut off before it is uploaded, so the shared trace does not begin or end at your front door. If a ride is short enough that trimming consumes the whole track, the ride is shared with no route line at all rather than an untrimmed one.
  • Forum posts, comments, votes, places you add, and reviews you write are visible to other signed-in riders and carry your name.

6. Live-ride sharing links

If you start live sharing during a ride, the app creates a session identified by a random 32-character token and gives you a link like https://throttleiqfb.web.app/live/<token>. While the ride is running, that session record holds your current latitude and longitude, your current speed, your phone's battery percentage, your ride status (riding, paused, crash, completed), the time it was last updated, your account ID, and the ride ID. It updates continuously as you ride, and it plots a live trail on a map.

Please understand what the link does

  • Anyone holding the link can watch — no account, no sign-in, no approval from you. The link is the permission. Whoever you send it to can forward it to anyone else, and it will work for them too.
  • Session records are stamped to expire 24 hours after the ride starts, and the viewer page refuses to display a session past that point. The record itself is not automatically deleted at expiry today — expiry stops the page from showing it, it does not erase it. Adding automatic deletion is a known outstanding item.
  • Only send a live link to people you actually want to know where you are, and treat it as public once it leaves your hands.

7. Crash detection

The app watches for a sudden impact combined with a sharp drop in speed. If it thinks you've crashed, it starts an on-screen countdown you can cancel. If you don't cancel it, the app records a crash alert containing your account ID, the ride ID, the time, and your last known coordinates.

ThrottleIQ is not an emergency service, and does not call one. The automatic SMS and email escalation to your emergency contacts is not live yet — the crash alert is recorded, but at this stage the app does not itself contact anybody on your behalf. Do not rely on ThrottleIQ as a safety net. The one thing that genuinely works today is the live-share link you send out yourself before or during a ride.

If you cancel a crash countdown, the app records that as a false positive against your own account, so detection thresholds can be improved.

8. How long data is kept

  • On your phone: rides, GPS trails, bikes and maintenance logs stay until you delete them in the app or uninstall it.
  • In the cloud: your profile, ride summaries, ride GPS trails, bikes, maintenance logs, routes, emergency contacts, posts, reviews and shared rides are kept for as long as your account exists. There is no automatic expiry on any of it.
  • Live-share sessions: stop being viewable 24 hours after they start; the underlying record persists until deleted (see §6).
  • Deleted content: when you delete a ride, post, review, or emergency contact in the app, the record is removed. Photos already uploaded to Cloudinary are not automatically removed when the post referencing them is deleted — email the address below if you need an image taken down.

9. Deleting your account and your data

There is currently no in-app "delete my account" button — it is being built. Until it ships, the process is:

  1. Email the.abraar.rar@gmail.com from the address you signed up with, asking for account deletion. Using the signup address is how the request is verified as yours.
  2. Your sign-in credential and every cloud record tied to your account is deleted: profile, username reservation, ride summaries, ride GPS trails, bikes, maintenance logs, saved routes, emergency contacts, badges, notifications, live sessions, and crash alerts. Uploaded photos are removed from Cloudinary.
  3. Content you posted in public — forum posts and replies, comments, place entries and reviews — is deleted on request too. Say so in your email if you want it removed; otherwise it may be left in place, detached from your profile, so threads other riders replied to don't break.
  4. Expect this to be handled within about 30 days. It is done by hand, by one person.

You can also delete most things yourself at any time without emailing anyone: individual rides, bikes, maintenance entries, emergency contacts, posts, and shared rides all have a delete action in the app. To wipe everything held locally, uninstall the app.

Want a copy of your data? Rides can be exported to JSON or GPX from the app. For anything else, email the address above and it will be sent to you.

10. Security, honestly stated

All traffic to Firebase, Cloudinary, OpenStreetMap, and Overpass goes over HTTPS. Access to the database is restricted by server-side security rules, so one rider cannot read another rider's private rides, bikes, maintenance logs, or emergency contacts. Sign-in is handled by Firebase Authentication; passwords are never stored by the app itself.

Two things you should weigh honestly: live-share links are readable by anyone who has the link, by design (§6); and this is a beta app maintained by one person, with no independent security audit and no formal breach-notification programme. If a breach affecting rider data is discovered, it will be reported by email to affected riders and noted on this page. Please make your own judgement about how much of your riding you want stored in it.

11. Children

ThrottleIQ is for licensed motorcycle riders and is not intended for children. Accounts are not knowingly created for anyone under 13, or under the minimum age of digital consent where you live if that is higher. If you believe a child has created an account, email the address below and it will be deleted.

12. Changes to this policy

If what the app collects changes, this page changes with it and the effective date at the top is updated. For a change that materially affects data already collected, notice will be given in the app before it takes effect.
